Bio-Vision: Submission Build Architecture

One build day, one layer of new code. Everything below serves a single claim: surgical simulation with no simulator — patient-specific anatomy training on any laptop with a webcam, so the institutions with the worst cadaver ratios aren't the ones priced out of the fix.

Written 13 Aug 2026, night Build day: 14 Aug Submission closes 15 Aug Deliverable: 5 slides + 5–7 min MP4

Why this document exists

The last three weeks added features. None of them were the problem, and none of them were the fix. This pins down what ships, what hides, and the one thing left to build.

The diagnosis, stated plainly: the platform is not under-built, it is under-evidenced. Simulation Effectiveness (25%) and Educational Impact (20%) together are 45% of the rubric, and both ask whether anyone actually learned anything. The 2025 healthcare winner answered that with nine nursing students and a before/after table. Bio-Vision currently answers it with zero.

So tomorrow builds a study instrument, not a feature. Everything else is selection and storytelling.

The study layer — architecture

The only new code. Designed to be purely additive: it reads the existing store and writes its own, so a bug here cannot break the demo.

Design constraints

  • Zero edits to engine.ts or store.ts. Both are working and demo-critical. The study layer only reads ProgressStore.attempts, which is already a public getter.
  • URL-gated, following the precedent already in surgilearn/index.ts where the anchor picker checks ?anchors. Study mode activates on ?study and is invisible otherwise — so the video demo and the research instrument never collide.
  • No server, no account. Data stays in localStorage until the researcher exports it. This is not a shortcut; it is the same zero-footprint claim the platform is built on, and it means running the study needs no IT approval.
  • Export early, export often. localStorage is one cleared cache away from losing your entire dataset.

The seam to the existing store

This is the entire integration surface. Two lines, both read-only:

// At practice start — remember where this participant's attempts begin.
session.practice.fromAttemptIndex = store.attempts.length;

// At practice end — everything recorded in between belongs to them.
session.practice.attempts = store.attempts.slice(session.practice.fromAttemptIndex);

Because ProgressStore.record() is the single funnel every completed case passes through, this slice is guaranteed complete without subscribing to anything. Do not call store.reset() between participants — it destroys history and the index arithmetic with it. Let attempts accumulate; the slice keeps them separated.

Wizard state machine

consent  ──▶ pre ──▶ practice ──▶ post ──▶ sus ──▶ done
   │                    │                            │
   │                    │ (app runs normally,        │ export CSV + JSON
   │                    │  panel collapses to        │ then "New participant"
   │                    │  a timer + END button)     │

During practice the study panel must get out of the way — collapse to a small floating timer with one END PRACTICE button. The participant is using the real app, unmodified, and anything else on screen contaminates both the experience and the SUS score they give you afterwards.

Export format

One CSV row per participant, plus a full JSON dump for anything the CSV flattens away. Trigger both with a single button via Blob + a temporary anchor — no library, no server.

participant, cohort, device, pre_score, post_score, gain,
conf_pre, conf_post, sus_score, cases_completed, mean_case_score,
mean_case_time_s, off_target_ids, practice_duration_s, notes

SUS scoring — get this right, it is easy to fumble

Odd-numbered items (1,3,5,7,9):  contribution = response − 1
Even-numbered items (2,4,6,8,10): contribution = 5 − response
susScore = sum(contributions) × 2.5     // → 0..100

68 is the published average across systems; above 68 is above average, above 80 is the "excellent" band. Put the 68 line on your chart — it is what makes the number legible to a judge who does not know the scale.

Instruments

Every quiz item is taught by content already in cases.ts — the REGION_INFO blurbs and the per-case teaching strings. That tight coupling between what the tool teaches and what the test measures is your construct-validity argument; say it out loud on the slide.

Knowledge probe — 5 items, forms A and B

  1. Which artery runs the anterior interventricular groove to the apex? → LAD
  2. A proximal occlusion of which vessel is termed the "widow-maker"? → LAD
  3. Which vessel gives off obtuse marginal branches to the lateral wall? → LCX
  4. A 40% luminal stenosis is managed with: → medical therapy, not revascularisation
  5. An RCA arising from the left coronary sinus with an interarterial course risks: → compression between aorta and pulmonary trunk on exertion

Form B is the same five items with option order shuffled and item order reversed. This controls for position memory but not for item familiarity — state that limitation rather than letting a judge find it.

Also captured, one line each

  • Confidence, 1–5: "How confident are you identifying the coronary arteries on a CT angiogram?" Asked before and after.
  • SUS, the standard 10 statements, 1–5. Do not rewrite them — the benchmark only holds for the validated wording.

Protocol — per participant, ~22 minutes

2 min

Setup & consent

Assign the next ID (P01, P02…). Read the one-paragraph consent aloud: what you're collecting, that it's anonymous, that they can stop. Record device and webcam type — variation here is data, not noise. It is direct evidence the thing runs on ordinary hardware.

4 min

Pre-test

Form A + confidence rating. No feedback, no scores shown. If they see which ones they missed, the post-test measures memory of the answer key rather than learning from the tool.

10 min

Practice — the intervention

CHALLENGE mode, Cases 1 → 2 → 3, hands only. You sit back and say nothing beyond fixing outright technical faults. Every word of coaching you give is a confound you will have to disclose. Note observations in the notes field instead of speaking.

5 min

Post-test + SUS

Form B, confidence again, then the 10 SUS statements. Export immediately — do not queue up three participants before the first export.

1 min

Reset for next

"New participant" clears the session but not the attempt history. Re-verify the export file grew.

Throughput

Two laptops running in parallel gets you 8 participants in about 90 minutes of wall-clock time. Recruit from your own cohort — classmates who have covered cardiac anatomy but are not cath-lab experts are exactly the right population, and "second/third-year BME students" is a defensible, honestly-stated sample.

Report honestly

With n≈8 and no control group this is a pilot, and calling it one costs you nothing — the 2025 winner reported n=9. Say "pilot study, n=8, single-arm pre/post, no control" on the slide. A judge who spots an overclaim discounts everything else you said; a judge who sees a clean limitation statement trusts the rest.

Ethics & honesty register

Two of these are unresolved in the repo right now. Both are cheap to state and expensive to be caught on.

  • Clinical content caveat. cases.ts carries a header warning that Cases 1 and 2 load patient-derived scans while their vignettes and answer keys were authored against reference anatomy. Either correct them tomorrow morning or state on the slide that the anatomy is patient-derived while the teaching vignettes are instructor-authored. Do not let this sit silently.
  • Depth is stylised. A single webcam gives relative depth, not metric depth. Your own code comments already say this. Saying it in the video reads as rigour, not weakness.
  • Anonymity. Participant IDs only, no names, nothing leaves the device until you export it.
  • Not a validated assessment. The score is a teaching signal, not a certification instrument. Say so.
